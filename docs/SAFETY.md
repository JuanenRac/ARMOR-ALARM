# Safety: what this project does and does not claim

- **This is not a certified alarm system.** It is a home project: its rules and its firmware are tested on a computer and in a browser against a stand-in node, and have never run on a board or on a real installation. Do not rely on it where a certified system, or a monitoring service, is required, and keep the protections the installation already has (smoke detectors that sound by themselves, for example).
- **A siren is loud.** It stops by itself after a time, and a correct PIN silences it at once without disarming (the alarm stays latched and recorded; a zone that has not sounded yet may sound it again); keep the time reasonable and respect the rules of where you live about sirens.
- **A wired loop and a siren relay carry low voltage.** Mains never goes on the node's board; whatever a relay switches is wired by someone qualified, with its own protection. See the base board's notes in ARMOR-NODE-ELECTRICAL.
- **A smoke detector or a panic button that is sounding is never left out** when arming is forced.
- **The PIN** is checked without leaking where it differs, and repeated wrong PINs lock the panel for a while. A PIN is only as private as the place it is typed and the way it is stored; the settings that hold it must be protected.
- **The server's commands are off by default.** The server may arm, disarm and silence the siren only when the node's setting, the broker's access list and the server's own switch all allow it; the server asks for the signed-in user's PIN before it sends a disarm or a silence, but the message to the node carries none, so whoever can publish on the broker's command topic can disarm or silence. Keep that permission to the server's account and the broker on a protected network.
- **A fault of the node is not an alarm.** A zone whose pin cannot be read reports nothing, and the siren relay starts open on every restart - and a node that has stopped is not guarding either. A restart does not hide an alarm that was ringing: the panel takes back the mode, the alarm (also one of smoke or panic with the system disarmed), whether its siren had already timed out, the zones left out and a lockout, and rings again when it was ringing. A node that has lost its converter reads no wired zone: it says so in the panel, and it cannot know the house is guarded.
- **A zone that cannot be read is not shown as closed.** If the converter does not start or a loop gives no reading for five rounds in a row, the zone is in `trouble`: the panel will not arm with it unless it is forced (and then the zone is left out and noted), and the server warns. Before this, such a zone stayed "normal" for ever and the house looked protected.
- **A reset by hand is refused while the alarm is armed.** Holding the BOOT button at power-up (or the factory reset of the panel's page) wipes the settings and the users only when the panel is disarmed; the saved state of the alarm is wiped with them. Whoever can open the box can still cut the power: use a box with a tamper switch and a siren with its own battery.
- **When the server is away** the panel decides alone. If the panel or the radio's node is also down, nobody guards: plan the power and, if it matters, a second radio.

## Arming profiles

A profile (see ALARM_MESSAGES.md) can make an intrusion silent and can leave zones out of one arming. It cannot make the house less safe in the ways this page promises: smoke, panic and tamper zones always guard and always sound, a tamper on a zone the profile leaves out still raises the alarm, the PIN and the lockout are the same, a zone in trouble still stops an arming, and a sounding always-zone is never left out by a forced arming. A silent profile is the owner's choice for a situation they know (a night in, a duress rehearsal); it is told to the server like any alarm. Nothing here has run on a real installation.
